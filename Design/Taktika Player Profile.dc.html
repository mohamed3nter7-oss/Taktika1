Player profile, public view

One job: a scout who does not know this player judges fit in about twenty seconds, then contacts him. Every element below is either evidence for that judgement or the contact action itself. Nothing on the page reports a number the platform does not hold — no goals, no appearances, no ratings.

Header density

Read order: name and role, then position and foot, then club and league level, then location and age, then followers. Position and foot outrank the administrative facts because they are what a scout filters on, and they are the only facts here no other role's profile carries. Followers land last at 13px — social proof, not evidence.

Physical attributes

Height, weight and shirt number are one right-rail block, not three header items. They are reference numbers a scout checks after he is interested, not facts he scans, and grouping them keeps three optional values out of the identity block. One present value renders one row; none renders no block.

Club history

A timeline, not cards. Cards would give three affiliations equal weight; a career is read top-down. Current club carries three signals and no extra colour: it is first, it holds the only badge in the list, and its range ends in "present".

Certifications

Credentials are reference, not narrative, so they sit in the rail and never compete with the timeline. Verified is a 14px accent check after the name; unverified shows nothing. Absence is the signal, which keeps the flag quieter than the role badge instead of racing it.

Two things to sign off

The header is composed from Card, Avatar, RoleBadge, PositionChip, ClubCrest and Button rather than the existing ProfileHeader, which has no props for headline, preferred foot or league level. Adding those three props to ProfileHeader makes this the component, not a one-off.

Avatars, post images and club crests are placeholders. Crests use the design system's initials fallback; drop real PNGs in and the light image plate takes over with no layout change.

01 Identity block, top to bottom

Name at 32px with the role badge on the avatar. Headline underneath in secondary — it is the player's own framing of what he wants, and it costs one line. Then the football row: role, primary position in accent, secondary position quiet, preferred foot after a hairline. Then club with crest and league level. Then city, country and age.

02 Actions sit on the card's last line

Message is the single filled accent button on the page; Follow is secondary beside it. Anchoring both to the bottom-right of the card keeps them in the same place on every profile, however many optional lines the header carries — a scout reading twenty profiles clicks without re-aiming.

03 The pitch line closes the header

One 1px accent rule at 24% alpha, bleeding to both card edges, directly under the identity block. It appears once on this page and nowhere else, and it is the whole athletic-personality budget.

04 Rail is persistent, tabs are not

Details and certifications sit in the 300px rail so they survive a move into About or Career. The portfolio link lives with them: it is an attribute, not an action, and putting it in the header would put a second competing target next to Message.

05 Posts is the default tab

It is the only surface on the page written by the player rather than assembled from fields, so it is the fastest read on character and intent. Text and images only — there is no video anywhere in the product.
1b Complete profile · About tab tabs are live — click through on any board
06 The one labelled surface

Header and rail are scan surfaces, so they carry facts without labels. About is the reference view: every attribute the API holds, spelled out with its label, in two columns. Bio at 17px on a 60-character measure above the divider.

07 No empty rows, ever

An absent field is absent — no em dash, no "Not specified", no greyed row. The grid reflows and the card gets shorter. See 1d for what that leaves.

08 Position codes stay Latin

ST and LW are wrapped LTR and isolated here and in the chips, so an Arabic layout mirrors around them without reordering the pair.
1c Complete profile · Career tab three affiliations, current first
09 Crest column doubles as the timeline

A 40px crest is the node and a subtle hairline connects it to the next one, so the spine costs no extra column. Club name at 17px, role at 15px, dates at 13px muted with an en dash — the club is what a scout recognises first, the years are what he checks second.

10 Current club, three quiet signals

First in the list, the only Current badge in the column, and the range ending in "present". No accent ring on the crest and no size change: the crest is user artwork and putting a system colour on it fights whatever the club's own palette is.

11 Certifications stay in the rail

Even on the Career tab, where they would be a plausible second list. Two lists in one column makes the scout decide which one matters; keeping credentials in the rail means the timeline is the only thing to read here.
1d Sparse profile · sixteen-year-old academy player no photo, headline, bio, measurements, certifications or posts
12 The header holds its shape

Every optional item is a whole row, never an inline fragment, so removing one closes a gap instead of re-flowing a sentence. What is left still answers the scout's first three questions: what he plays, which foot, what level. The initials avatar is the design system's own fallback, not a grey silhouette.

13 The rail column keeps its width

With no measurements and no certifications there is nothing to put in it, and it stays empty on purpose. Collapsing it would move the identity block sideways, and a scout opening twenty profiles in a session would get a different page shape every time. The empty rail is the cheaper cost.

14 Empty posts, seen by a visitor

The owner's version of this state offers Create post. A visitor has no action here, so it offers none — one line naming the space, then a quiet link to the one tab that does have content. The tab counts do the same work: 0 and 1 tell him where to look before he clicks.

15 Age 16, and only age

No date, no birthday, no age band, at any age. The header reads "Age 16" rather than a bare number so it can never be mistaken for a shirt number.
1e Own profile · the same player viewing himself Follow and Message become Edit profile
16 One action in, two actions out

Edit profile takes the filled accent slot Message had, in the same position, so the rule holds at one primary action per view. Nothing else changes: no edit affordances on individual fields, no completeness meter, no owner-only rail card. The owner sees exactly what a scout sees, which is the only reliable way to know how he reads.

17 Open questions

Should the owner get an inline prompt when the profile is as sparse as 1d, or does that belong in onboarding? And does a scout need Save to shortlist next to Message — it is not in the field list, but it is the action a scout takes twenty times a session.
